Spoon and Saucer

Stir a cup of tea and the leaves on the bottom creep into a heap in the middle. Turn the cup instead and they slide out into a ring against the wall. Both are real, and both come from the same thing: the tea at the bottom of a cup is slowed by the floor, the tea above it is not, and the slow layer flows toward wherever the cup is moving faster than the tea.

Stirring spins the tea faster than the cup. The floor drags the bottom layer back, the pressure above pushes it in, and the leaves are carried to the middle. Turning the cup does the opposite: the cup is faster than the tea, so the bottom flows outward. The leaves on the floor go with it.

How to play

  • Drag in circles inside the cup to stir. Let go and wait: the leaves gather.
  • Drag around the saucer to turn the cup. Let go: it coasts, and the leaves move out. The ring is not permanent; once the tea catches up, the leaves drift back in.
  • Double-tap the tea to tip the leaves back into a scatter. Keyboard: left and right arrows stir; Shift with them turns the cup.

What is real here, and what isn't

The tea's response uses water's viscosity, the cup's size and the spin, with no other tuning, except one choice: the cup coasts to a stop with a 15 second time constant. How easily a leaf slides on china is not well known, so each of the 160 leaves draws its own value from the range in which a real cup does make a heap. The tea's surface dip is exaggerated ten times so you can see it. The floating leaves are a picture, not a calculation. Leaves can't overlap, and they pack into a heap about a third of the cup wide.

Honest limits: a slow, lazy stir does little; the heap forms at a brisk stir. Nobody outside the studio has tried this work yet, so it has not been read by a fresh visitor.
